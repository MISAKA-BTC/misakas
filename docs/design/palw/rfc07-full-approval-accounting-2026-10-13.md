# RFC07 full approval accounting — 2026-10-13

The historical `ApprovedTupleV1` codec states relation-family soundness and a finite retry/grinding/query strategy budget. It does not state commitment binding, source failure, or missing-scope probabilities. Its evaluator previously returned approval based on algebraic bits alone, including `Complete` when arithmetic enumerated the whole domain. Complete enumeration cannot make a hash commitment exact or establish a source participation floor.

The repaired `approved_v1` keeps the old codec and its malformed/weak-tuple diagnostics, but refuses full approval without those review terms. `ReviewedTupleV2` keeps the old tuple nested unchanged and adds its full policy descriptor, binding/source/coverage bounds and a review digest. Each bound explicitly says `Unknown`, finite whole bits, or `Exact`; unknown contributes probability one. No omitted event defaults to exact.

`approved_v2` evaluates the trusted release registry's declarations. Binding and coverage pay the same retries, grinding and adaptive-query horizon as algebraic checks. Source failures additionally pay the number of beacons per attempt. It unions the four events without independence, then unions every authorized tuple in the supplied registry, including other suites and policy versions. Two otherwise exact tuples with distinct 128-bit binding events have a 127-bit registry bound. A descriptor substitution, an absent review digest, an unknown source, or insufficient global bits refuses approval. Both shipped registries remain empty.

The registry must include the whole declared lifetime, including previously authorized policy versions. A caller supplying only a new registry is not evidence that historical events disappeared. The evaluator cannot authenticate an external review, prove a source floor, or enforce a live lifetime admission counter. No consensus consumer currently calls the approval registry APIs; onboarding still uses its explicit unapproved mechanics policies and existing counted per-class retry record. This change prevents the approval API from silently overstating its input; it does not close authenticated lifetime budgets, independent 128-bit review, source independence/censorship, paid-source profitability, or all adapter/real-node acceptance requirements.
