## Cancellation and bounded recovery

A canceled carrier future owns and drops the drained raw batch. The source sidecar retains its exact independently admitted packet, original snapshot and due date. On the next real retry-buffer preparation, all outstanding source copies and destination metadata/array/map capacity are admitted before changing logical pending/due entries. A local refusal leaves the complete original sidecar in flight and defers the flush; it cannot prune the last copy. Successful recovery restores an outstanding packet once. The same old snapshot is then freshly checked at the carrier doors.

The actual success and terminal-stale paths mark that exact source packet finished synchronously. Such packets are excluded from canceled-flight recovery and released after their pending payload retires; successful packets cannot be resurrected merely because a later packet's future was canceled. Ordinary transport/read retries preserve the marker as unfinished. This is in-process cancellation recovery, not restart recovery or persisted authority.

The shared `try_push_clone_with_due` API preadmits retained due-map capacity and payload-copy storage before inserting either logical queue entry. The held-GDN caller separately reuses this API; this document does not claim its execution or court outcome coverage.
