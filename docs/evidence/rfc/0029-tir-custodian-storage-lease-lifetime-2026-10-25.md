The actual TIR custodian opened its cold capture reader before taking the quoted host reservation. During a pass it moved its reader/writer into a blocking worker while their reservations stayed in the async book. Dropping a cancelled caller/book could therefore release quota while detached blocking work still owned the storage. The response-body worker and maintenance reopen likewise held their host guards outside the blocking closure.

The production capture-open caller now resolves the original source and existing backend need, then uses `CustodianOwnedV2::try_build` to reserve before invoking capture open. Its reader and the prepared writer each move as one storage/guard wrapper through the actual pass worker and its result back into the book. Wrapper fields drop storage before quota. Backend failures release the reservation; a host-capacity refusal never invokes open and remains an existing local pending error, rather than accepted-state absence, a signer fault or a conviction.

The response caller obtains the backend's existing exact prepared-reader reopen quote and checked-adds `reopen_ram_bytes` to the existing request-body material RAM quote before opening. The combined host guard moves into the worker, remains with its body output, and is released after that body. Retaining the full combined ticket after the short-lived reader drops is conservative. Maintenance moves its reopen ticket inside the blocking closure. This addresses logical quoted need and lifetime; it does not claim measurement of mmap residency or complete allocator coverage.
