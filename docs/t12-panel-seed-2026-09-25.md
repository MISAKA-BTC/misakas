# testnet-12 lane F1: the panel seed, behind a post-launch fence (2026-09-25)

A correction to ADR-0152 v3.1 SW-8, shipped after launch. SW-8 prices the anchor re-roll at "one
block's proof of work per try" and, after M4, "one inference per try". Neither held. The ADR file lives
outside this repository, so the correction is recorded here and should be copied into ADR-0152 §3.14
SW-8 ("A seed-time lever that stays" and "SW-8 as integrated").

testnet-12 launched from `0e8ec984e` with the rule described under "What was wrong". This lane does
not change that release. It adds a fence, `Params::palw_panel_seed_execution`, which is dormant
(`None`) on every shipped preset, testnet-12 included. An operator arms it at a post-launch height.

## What was wrong (`wf_72c1a397-e23`, CRITICAL)

The panel seed was the anchor block's identity hash. That one seed keyed three draws:

* the panel (`palw_panel_seat_ticket_v1` and the stake race);
* the segment assignment (`palw_segment_assignment_v2`);
* the S3 sample (`palw_layer_sample_v3`).

The class lottery prices none of what goes into that identity:

* The identity covers `palw_commitment`'s raw bytes, and those include the attempt's ML-DSA-87
  signature.
* It also covers the timestamp and the full nonce.
* `execution_commitment_v3` blanks the challenge.
* The pre-PoW hash zeroes the nonce and the timestamp, and it leaves out the commitment.
* The nonce counts only through its 2^22 bucket.

So one lottery win gave the anchor's producer as many valid block identities as it wanted. It could
re-sign (about 1.2 ms), move the timestamp, or move the nonce inside its bucket. Each variant drew a
different panel, and the producer saw each panel privately before it published anything.

Measured:

* **A coverage lie reached `Final` with 0 slash.** It took 2 Sybil seats (260k MSK) and about 1,000
  re-signs.
* **Silent-coalition panels pushed an honest producer into S0′.**

## What lane F1's fence changes

For a claim whose **anchor block's DAA score** is at or past the fence:

    seed = H("misaka-palw/panel-v2/draw-seed/v1" ‖ anchor attempt's execution_commitment_v3 ‖ claim)

(`palw_panel_v2::palw_panel_draw_seed_v1`). Below the fence the seed is the anchor block, byte for byte.

* **Where the value comes from.** The processor's one anchor walk (`palw_v2_anchor_fact_of_candidate`
  → `palw_v2_anchor_fact_with_seed_v1`) reads the execution commitment off the anchor block's own header
  (`palw_panel_anchor_execution_v1`). It is the same value as the block's fold key and the input the
  class ticket is drawn from.
* **Who uses it.** The chain's derivation and the acceptance gate both get the anchor fact from that
  one walk, so they cannot disagree.
* **What is stored.** A `PanelBound` names the seed as its `anchor`, the gate demands it, and the fold
  stores it as `PalwPanelStateV2::anchor`. The segment assignment, the S3 sample, the S2 optimistic
  door and the leaf-evidence interval draw all read that stored field, so all of them are keyed on the
  one seed. Nothing treats `panel.anchor` as a block hash.

**The switch key is the anchor's DAA score.**

* The gate already resolves the whole draw policy there: stake, the Valid lock, maturity, capability.
* The anchor header is chain data that every node holds. The anchor walk already finds it, so reorg,
  IBD and a pruning-proof sync all resolve the rule the same way.
* A claim whose slot is below the fence and whose anchor is past it takes the new rule. The processor
  test pins this case as claim C.

**Prerequisite.** `validate_palw_v2` refuses the fence without `palw_rcore_plus` at or below it. Past
R-core+ only attempt blocks anchor, so every anchor has an execution commitment. testnet-12 arms R-core+
at genesis, so any post-launch height is legal there.

**Fence mechanics.** The fence follows the repo's pattern:

* the field;
* the `palw_fences_v1` list (the fork id and the schedule walk read it);
* the `for_each_fence` visitor (Some-only);
* the Some-only writes in `consensus_schedule_id` and `consensus_params_id`. The params id writes the
  seed rule's domain beside the height, so the rule's version rides its fence;
* the `Some(never())` → `None` collapse in `normalize_values_a_scheduled_fence_drags_with_it`;
* `override_params` carries it, beside R-core+;
* `fork_id_v1`'s probe and the extension's ruleset candidate.

Armed at a future height, the fence moves `consensus_params_id` and `consensus_schedule_id`. It does
not move `consensus_identity_id`, so an armed node and a shipped node stay peers until the height.

**Choosing the height.** Pick one independent height for all post-launch fences, and do not pick
1,000. That is testnet-12's `palw_bond_maturity` height, and a fence placed at an already-scheduled
height is invisible to the fork id. At the height, the armed build refuses a node that did not upgrade.
That refusal is named, not a silent fork.

**Fingerprints.** Dormant, the fence changes no fingerprint. testnet-12 stays `b8564b88…` /
`5de80e64…` / `93da24cc…`, and every other preset is unmoved.

## What the fence does and does not buy — read this before relying on it

**What it closes.** Every identity that one lottery win can take draws one panel. That covers any
signature, any timestamp and any nonce in the bucket. The panel changes only if the execution commitment
changes, and a new execution commitment means a new lottery win.

**What it does not close while audit P0-10 is open.** The header door does not check an attempt's trace
root against an inference, so a lottery win costs about 279 junk BLAKE2b draws on the testnet-12 floor
(`palw_expected_attempts_v1` of the floor's effective target).

* That is tens of microseconds, less than the ML-DSA re-signature it replaces. The panel derivation
  (~1–2 ms, debug) was already the dominant cost of a draw, before and after.
* The regression prints this residual. `void_after_panel_adv_verify` a1 finds a segment-0 Sybil pair
  in 2 of 1,000 fresh anchor attempts, about 0.002 per lottery win.
* **So the attacker's cost to capture a panel barely moves until lottery wins cost inferences.**

The fence is necessary: without it, an inference-bound lottery would still leave the identity re-roll
free. It is not sufficient on its own.

## (2) "A seed the anchor producer cannot evaluate before it publishes": why no seed rule does it

Every seed rule has a **last contributor**: the party whose input is fixed last. It knows every other
input when it fixes its own. So it can evaluate the seed before it publishes, withhold what it dislikes,
and try another input. How far it can re-roll depends on what an alternative input costs it.

Candidate designs, and what each one moves:

| design | who can still preview | what a re-roll costs them | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seal**: mix in the execution of the first attempt block at or past `anchor_daa + D`, and bind there | the seal's producer | one lottery win (as F1 prices the anchor) | moves the preview from the anchor to the seal and changes nothing against a strategic attacker, who targets the seal instead. It also adds D DAA to every bind, needs SW-8's bind block and step 4c's void re-keyed, and is a new liveness surface |
| **Window aggregate**: mix in every attempt execution in W DAA after the anchor | whoever publishes the last attempt before the window closes. The closing tick can be stamped up to 132 s early under the lead cap, and a block with old parents can still carry a window DAA | one lottery win per extra attempt | same as the seal |
| **Bond-keyed input** (the bond and DAA of the anchor, not its content) | everyone, in advance | a re-roll needs another bond (capital) | the claimant knows the input when it makes its claim, and grinds its claim id with junk attempts (P0-10) until the panel is captured. Worse |
| **Claimant commit–reveal** (the claim commits `H(r)`; seed = `H(exec ‖ claim ‖ r)`; bind at the reveal; a withheld reveal forfeits, per the user's principle) | the claimant | a forfeit per withheld reveal | closes the VICTIM case (a non-author anchor producer can no longer grief an honest claim), but not the main attack, where the claimant and the anchor producer are one party. Needs an attempt-format change, a reveal object and the bind moved. Days |
| **Per-bond pre-committed randomness** (hash-chain reveal) or a VRF | the claimant, for its own bonds' future values | one bond | claim-id grinding again while P0-10 is open. Registration and envelope changes. Days |
| **Delay function** | nobody, if the delay exceeds the grinding window | — | every validator would evaluate it for every bind, and ASIC speed-ups break the bound. Not sound here |

**The deciding fact is P0-10.**

* With junk wins at about 279 hashes, an attacker can make itself the first attempt block at any slot
  it chooses. It can publish within milliseconds of the slot-reaching heartbeat, while honest producers
  need seconds for an inference. It can also mint the slot-reaching heartbeat itself up to 132 s ahead
  (probe T6).
* It can also grind its own claim id for free.
* So every chain position after the claim is attacker-ownable, and so is the claim. No mix of
  positions contains entropy the attacker cannot choose.

The user's principle ("from the moment a producer can learn whether an outcome favours it, withdrawing
must not remove its obligation") cannot bite on an unpublished candidate: nothing was accepted, so
nothing can carry an obligation. It bites only if evaluating a candidate first requires committing it
on chain. That commitment must be fixed by a later input that the committer does not control, and on
this chain that input is again attacker-ownable.

**Decision: no seed-only rule is implemented for (2).** A seal would satisfy the letter ("the anchor
producer cannot evaluate") while changing nothing an attacker pays, and it would add a bind-block change
and a liveness risk to a fence that has to ship within a day. The probes therefore prove the part that
is true: the identity grind is impossible past the fence. They also print the part that is not closed:
the per-win capture rate.

## (3) P0-10 at the header door — assessment

Closing P0-10 means a lottery ticket that costs an inference to produce and is cheap to check. The ADR
states that this needs "a ticket that is a function of a certified execution (ADR-0141 §2), not feasible
in this regenesis" (§3.9).

At the header door every node would have to verify an inference, which means model weights and a
re-execution or sampled layer check on every attempt. PALW verifies after the fact, through panels. So
P0-10 is not a contained change under this fence.

The options below are for the user. Option 0 fits in 24–30 h. None of the others does.

0. **Operator-anchored panels on testnet-12: a stopgap, not a P0-10 fix.**
   * Past the fence, only an attempt by one of testnet-12's genesis bonds may anchor a panel. That
     is one predicate in `palw_block_may_anchor_a_panel_v1`, which reads the header's executor bond.
     The anchor walk, step 4c and the one-state pre-check all read that predicate, so they stay
     consistent by construction.
   * An attacker can then no longer own the anchor position.
   * The claimant cannot predict the operator's future execution commitment, so grinding a claim id
     is useless.
   * The panel is then a fair draw, unless someone can reorg the operator's anchor block off the
     selected chain. That is the HB-transparency reorg lane's surface.
   * **The cost is permissionless anchoring.** Operator trust is added to the draw, and panels stop
     binding if the operator's producers are down for a whole bind window (the claims void S0, with no
     forfeit).
   * The user chose it on 2026-09-26 01:00 JST. It is implemented as lane A below
     (`Params::palw_operator_anchor`), with one correction to the sketch above: "the first operator
     attempt on the chain" alone is a race a non-operator can enter after reading the attempt's panel,
     so the seed is read off the earliest operator attempt in the anchor's past.

1. **The certified-execution ticket or seed (the ADR's own direction).**
   * A seed contributor, or a lottery ticket, counts only once its execution is certified.
   * **Seed variant:** the anchor must be an attempt whose own claim reached licence. This couples every
     bind to another claim's verification (latency, and a dependency chain), and it needs a bootstrap
     rule.
   * **Ticket variant:** ADR-0141 §2. Weeks.
2. **Panel-independent conviction** (X10 armed, plus operator-run non-seat DA filers auditing every
   claim).
   * A captured panel then stops being enough, because a junk claim still meets an honest accuser
     (DA-8 → S1/S2).
   * This is node policy plus X10's arming criterion (§3.9), not a new consensus rule. Of these, it is
     the fastest real backstop.
3. **Coverage basis of 3** (§9.2). Raises the Sybil count a coverage lie needs. It does not stop
   grinding, it only scales the number of tries.
4. **Announce-then-reveal anchors** (MSK-priced candidates). Days. It prices only published
   candidates unless a later uncontrolled input exists, and none does while P0-10 is open.

**Recommendation.**

* **Arm F1 at the common post-launch height.** It is necessary, and it is cheap.
* **If the user accepts operator trust on testnet-12 until an inference-bound ticket exists, add
  option 0 under the same height.** It is the only change inside the window that actually removes the
  attacker's anchor.
* **Treat the panel-capture CRITICAL as open until an inference-bound ticket exists.**
* **Before external seats mature, make (2) of the list above true on the fleet:** automatic non-seat DA
  filing on every claim by operator nodes. Keep O-2's panel-composition monitoring, and add
  anchor-producer ↔ seated-operator correlation.
* **Re-evaluate P\* with the anchor holder's capture ≈ 1.** That holds whether or not F1 is armed.
* **Schedule the certified-execution ticket** as the structural fix.

## Determinism

The seed depends only on consensus data every node holds: the anchor header (the headers store carries
`palw_commitment`) and the claim id. The switch reads the anchor header's DAA score. So reorg, IBD and a
pruning-proof sync recompute the same seed.

A header whose envelope does not decode names no anchor, and the claim waits out its bind window. The
header stage refuses such a block, so no chain block does this.

## What a live drill across the fence must check

Run it with the binary that ships, with testnet-12's lane profile, and with the fence at a low height
that the drill actually crosses.

1. **Handshake.**
   * Below the height, an armed node and a shipped node stay peers in both directions, with a
     `consensus_schedule_id` warning only.
   * At the height, the armed node refuses the shipped one by fork id (`NextFenceDiffers`). It must not
     fork silently.
2. **Identical chain below the fence.** Blocks, PALW state roots and panels match a shipped-binary
   node's.
3. **The first claim anchored at or past the height.**
   * Its stored `panel.anchor` equals `H(domain ‖ execution_commitment_v3(anchor header) ‖ claim)`,
     recomputed off-node from the anchor header. It is not the anchor block hash.
   * Seats see that seed through `getPalwNodeStatus`, the duty view and the segment assignment, and
     their segment and S3 sites follow it.
   * Receipts license: `ReceiptLicensed` → `Final` on at least one floor claim and one 8k claim past the
     fence.
4. **A straddling claim.** Slot below the height, anchor past it: it binds with the new seed. No
   `BindTimeout` spike and no `AnchorMismatch` or `PanelMismatch` in the logs at the crossing.
5. **Liveness through the crossing.**
   * The DAA clock keeps ticking (CLOCK_GATE).
   * Binds keep happening in anchor blocks.
   * Seats keep receipting.
6. **Sync and restart.**
   * A node restarted mid-crossing, and a fresh node doing IBD across the height, both end on the same
     sink and state root.
   * A reorg that swaps the anchor across the height re-derives the panel. At least one deep-reorg
     case, if the drill harness can force one.
7. **Evidence** comes from the run's own logs after the crossing, not from echoed configuration.

## Lane A: the operator-anchored panel (2026-09-26)

The user's decision (01:00 JST): F1 plus a stopgap — (A) past a fence only attempts produced by the
eight genesis bonds may anchor a claim; (B) operator nodes that are not seats file DA on claims (node
lane); (C) monitor which operators are seated under which anchor producers (node lane). This section
is (A), on branch `rcore/f1-panel-seed` above F1.

### The rule

New field `Params::palw_operator_anchor: Option<PalwOperatorAnchorV1 { activation, operators }>`
(`consensus/core/src/palw_operator_anchor_v1.rs`). `operators` is a sorted, distinct list of bonds the
genesis registers. On testnet-12 it is all eight cards
(`Params::palw_operator_anchor_of_genesis_bonds_v1`: premine `5e0d5f1b…` outputs 0..7).

* **What an operator attempt is.** An attempt whose envelope names an operator bond as executor under
  that bond's genesis-registered key (`PalwOperatorAnchorRuleV1::operator_of_v1`). The header stage
  already verified the envelope's signature against that key on every stored header, so the test reads
  only the header and the genesis registry. An operator's outpoint under another key is not an
  operator's attempt.
* **Which block anchors a claim.** Past the fence, resolved at the candidate chain block's OWN DAA
  score (the key F1 resolves the seed at): a chain block anchors a claim with slot `s` iff it IS, or
  MERGES (blue or red), an operator attempt whose DAA score is at or past `s`. The block's own lane does
  not matter. The claim's anchor is the first such chain block at or past `s`; it binds there (SW-8),
  or voids there if the draw refuses (S0), or re-anchors past the registry-resilience fence (V03).
  - One processor answer carries this: `VirtualStateProcessor::palw_chain_block_as_anchor_v1`. The
    anchor walk, step 4c and the one-state pre-check all read it, so they agree by construction.
  - A block anchors only the slots at or below the latest operator attempt it is or merges, capped at
    its own DAA score (its **reach**). Step 4c receives it as `PalwTransitionExtrasV1::sw8_anchor_reach`
    (`None` means the block's own DAA, which is the rule below the fence and on every other network).
    So a block that merges an operator attempt from before a claim's slot neither binds nor voids that
    claim.
* **What a non-operator attempt, or a heartbeat, at the slot does.** If it merges no operator attempt at
  or past the slot: nothing. It binds nothing and voids nothing (step 4c does not run for that slot).
* **Bounded by the bind window.** A slot that no operator attempt reaches (as a chain block or merged by
  one) before `bind_base + window_bind` voids `BindTimeout` at that backstop (S0, no forfeit), as a slot
  no attempt at all reached before.
* **Where the seed and the draw's clock come from.** From the EARLIEST operator attempt at or past the
  slot that the anchor is or merges, earliest in the DAG: one that no other of them is in the past of,
  and among concurrent ones the least `(DAA score, hash)`
  (`VirtualStateProcessor::palw_operator_seed_source_v1`). The DAA score cannot order them: on
  testnet-12 only heartbeats advance the clock, so an operator's next attempt, built on (and merging)
  a displaced one, carries the same DAA score, and a `(DAA score, hash)` minimum would have handed the
  seed to whichever hashed lower (a fresh draw on half of such displacements; the first run of the
  regression below passed by that coin, the second failed on it). The anchor fact's `anchor_daa`,
  which is the DAA score at which every draw input is resolved (the draw policy with its readiness
  clock, the maturity floor, the capability bound, the shard plan; by the chain's derivation and by the
  gate alike), is that attempt's DAA score. `anchor_block` stays the binding block (SW-8), and the F1
  and lane-A fences are resolved at the binding block's DAA. Undisturbed, all of this is the operator
  attempt itself. The draw's STATE is the binding block's pre-object base, as before.
* **Prerequisites** (`validate_palw_v2`): ConsensusV2, `palw_rcore_plus` at or below the activation,
  and F1 (`palw_panel_seed_execution`) at or below it. The seed must be an execution commitment:
  without F1 it would be the binding block's identity. The value checks: non-empty, sorted and
  distinct, every bond genesis-registered.

### Displacement, and why the anchor is "is or merges"

Anyone can race an operator attempt after the fact:

1. An operator's attempt O at or past the slot is published. Its execution commitment is in its header,
   so anyone can compute O's panel for every claim waiting on it (1–2 ms per claim).
2. A non-operator that does not like the panel releases a sibling P that the network prefers as selected
   parent. Siblings on the same parents have equal blue work (a block's own work counts only in its
   children), so P wins by the hash tie-break (50%, and a sibling can be ground in a few hundred BLAKE2b
   under P0-10), or outright by carrying one extra withheld blue parent.
3. O is no longer a chain block. The next chain block merges it.

Three rules were considered:

* **"First operator attempt on the selected chain", seed = that attempt.** Each displacement is a fresh
  draw at the next operator attempt: hundreds per 580-DAA bind window, and a segment-0 pair with two
  Sybils needs about 500 (a1). Rejected in the first lane A commit.
* **Bind at the first operator attempt on the selected chain, seed = the earliest operator attempt in
  its past** (lane A as first committed, bb4dc6d94). The verification (2026-09-26) found two faults:
  - *Finding 1 (medium).* A bonded non-operator that displaces EVERY operator attempt (about 0.4–0.9
    per DAA) holds every claim off its anchor until the backstop voids it `BindTimeout`: external
    producers' claims and the operator's own, each voided claim's escrowed worker reward destroyed. The
    probe (`verify/laneA-opanchor-probe`) displaced 15 operator attempts from slot to backstop, and the
    claim voided at DAA 603. The lever was new with lane A: under F1 alone the displacing attempt
    anchored the claim itself.
  - *Finding 2 (medium, plausible).* The seed was fixed by the displaced attempt, but the policy,
    readiness clock and maturity floor were resolved at the binding block, which the displacer could
    push up to the whole bind window away, after seeing the panel.
* **Bind at the first chain block that IS or MERGES an operator attempt at or past the slot; seed and
  draw clock = the earliest such attempt** (this rule). Displacing O moves the binding to the very next
  chain block, whoever produces it (a heartbeat, the displacer's own next attempt, another operator's
  attempt), on O's seed and at O's DAA. It cannot hold a claim off its anchor: to keep O out of the next
  chain block's past the displacer must also win that race against honest blocks that merge O, which
  carry more blue work, so it needs one more withheld blue parent per block. That is a sustained
  selfish-mining race against every honest producer, not a tie-break per operator attempt.

**What is left** (after this rule; each costs a won race and is visible to monitoring):

* **(a) Concurrent operator attempts.** Operator attempts neither of which is in the other's past are
  ordered by `(DAA score, hash)`; only operators make them. A block that merges both could, if an
  attacker produced it, include one and leave the other out, choosing between two operator seeds; that
  needs two operator attempts in flight at once and a won race.
* **(b) The draw's state.** The seed and the draw's clock are O's, but the draw reads the binding block's
  pre-object base, so a displacement changes the state the draw sees by one block: the displacer's own
  attempt (a claim on its bond), the objects the displacer's block accepts (its parents' bodies) and
  step 2 of the binding block (payouts, deadline sweeps, redraws). Honest seats' eligibility is not
  static: room tracks committed exposure, which moves as their own claims are accepted and reach Final
  and as other claims bind earlier in the same block (the attacker's own pending claims among them,
  whose ids it grinds); readiness rows lapse (now judged at O's DAA, so a lapse between O and the
  binding block no longer counts). So a won race buys **one alternative state for the same seed and
  clock**, and further alternatives only by keeping O out of further chain blocks' past as above.
  For a claim raced once that is at most about double the capture rate (two correlated draws on nearly
  the same eligible set), not the hundreds of fresh draws the first rule of this section allowed. How
  much eligibility churns between two adjacent blocks on live testnet-12 is not measured; in the harness
  (no churn) the seats were identical in all five displacement rounds. The attacker's own Sybils (taking
  them out of the draw, or timing a bond's maturity) remain the correlated `2^n − 1` variants of one seed
  for n Sybils noted before. An attacker holding enough of the eligible stake could also make the draw
  refuse at the binding block (SW-10's `InsufficientEligibleStake`, by loading its own seats in the
  displacing block) and so void its own claim S0 after seeing O's panel; against the operator's eight
  genesis bonds that takes a large share of all eligible stake, but the P* re-evaluation should count it.
* **(c) One fair draw per claim**, with a 2-Sybil pair rate of about 0.002 (from a1). Lane B (DA filers)
  and the P* re-evaluation should treat (c) and (b) as live.

The draw is not moved onto the displaced attempt's own state: that state is not computed by a node
whose chain never held the attempt, and a panel drawn on one state and locked on another could be
refused by the fold, which would turn displacement back into a void. The structural fix (a
certified-execution ticket) removes the need for the operator position altogether.

### Other ways a non-operator might game it, and why they do not work

* **Grinding its own attempts at the slot** (the P0-10 residual F1 left): none of them anchors
  (`void_after_panel_probe` T3c: 0 of 4,096 fresh junk wins; `void_after_panel_adv_verify` a5: 0 of
  1,000, and the capturing panel a win finds under F1 alone is refused by the gate against the
  operator's anchor). An attacker block anchors only by merging an operator attempt, and then the
  operator attempt seeds it.
* **Grinding its claim id:** the claim is fixed at acceptance, before any operator attempt at or past
  its slot exists, and the operator's future execution commitment is unpredictable. One fair draw per
  claim; a5 prints its pair rate (the per-CLAIM residual, which costs the attacker a claim that faces
  an honest panel and the non-seat DA filers when it is not captured).
* **Producing or withholding heartbeats to move the DAA clock:** moves which operator attempt reaches
  the slot first, but only before that attempt exists. Its execution is unknown until it is published,
  and its DAA is fixed by its own parents.
* **Withholding operator attempts:** only operators produce them.
* **Displacing its own claim's anchor to escape the panel** (withdrawal after observation): the claim
  binds in the next block on the same seed and clock (round 3 of the regression). It cannot void it.
* **Impersonation** (an operator's outpoint under another key): the test compares the carried key with
  the genesis key; the header stage verified the signature against the carried key.

### Composition

* **F1:** required at or below lane A; recommended at the same height. Past both, the seed is
  `H(execution of the earliest operator attempt at or past the slot that the anchor is or merges ‖
  claim)`.
* **Registry resilience (`rcore/f1-registry-resilience`, V03/V05):** its re-anchor runs inside step 4c,
  which runs only where `sw8_anchor_delay` is `Some`: past lane A, only at a block that is or merges an
  operator attempt. The re-based claim's next slot is found by the same walk, and the backstop is
  unchanged. **Merge note:** that branch computes its step-2e list `ncp_at_draw` with
  `palw_claims_provisional_past_their_anchor_slot_v1(&builder.state, ctx.daa_score, anchor_delay)` and
  its `palw_void_claims_unbound_in_their_anchor_block_v1` takes an extra `ncp_at_draw` argument; lane A
  now passes step 4c a `reach`. When the two merge, `ncp_at_draw` must be taken over the same
  `reach` (`extras.sw8_anchor_reach.map_or(ctx.daa_score, |r| r.min(ctx.daa_score))`) and the void
  function takes both arguments. Its retry re-bases on `ctx.daa_score` (the binding block), which is
  correct as is. One more thing to review at that merge: its "could not seat a panel" test reads the
  binding block's DAA, while lane A's draw clock is the seed source's (earlier by the displacement
  gap). A retry is taken only when the draw refused, and the draw no longer sees a readiness lapse in
  the gap, so the gap can matter only when the draw refused for another reason while rows lapsed in it;
  reading that test at the anchor fact's `anchor_daa` would close it. They need not share a height for correctness, but all post-launch fences should take
  one common independent height (one flag day, one drill), never 1,000. Otherwise the textual overlap
  is adjacent additions in `params.rs`'s four fence places, the fork-id probe and the extension's
  candidate list.
* **HB-transparency / other lanes:** lane A reads whatever mergesets the chain has, so a lane that
  changes what a heartbeat may merge does not change lane A's rule. A heartbeat that merges an operator
  attempt anchors its claims.

### Liveness when operator attempts are sparse

testnet-12: `anchor_delay` 20, `window_bind` 600 (so 580 DAA of slack after a slot, about 19 h at 120 s
a DAA), `window_challenge` 120, a genesis floor seat holds 64 concurrent floor claims.

* **What counts.** A claim binds once an operator attempt at or past its slot reaches the selected
  chain's past: as a chain block, or merged by one. Displacing the attempt from the chain does not stop
  that; only keeping it out of every chain block's past does, which means out-producing every honest
  block that merges it (above). So liveness is set by operator attempts PRODUCED and published, not by
  how many of them win the selected-parent race.
* **Supply.** The operator's producers are b1 and b6 (floor) and b0 (8k). A floor seat at capacity
  produces as fast as its claims Final: a claim lives about `20 (anchor wait) + a few (licence) + 120
  (challenge)` ≈ 145 DAA, so ≈ 64/145 ≈ 0.44 attempts per DAA per floor seat, ≈ 0.9 per DAA for b1+b6
  (less if the lottery, not capacity, is the limit).
* **Latency added.** A claim now waits for the next operator attempt after its slot: at 0.9 per DAA that
  is about one DAA (two minutes), plus one block if that attempt is displaced. Today every producer on
  testnet-12 is a genesis card, so nothing changes until external producers register; their claims bind
  at operator attempts.
* **Void risk.** At a Poisson rate λ of published operator attempts, P(none in 580 DAA) = e^(−580λ):
  2.5×10⁻¹³ even at λ = 0.05 (one per 40 minutes). The real risk is correlated: the operator's producers
  down, or all at capacity, for 19 h. Then claims void S0 at their backstops, which frees capacity. A
  majority block producer that keeps every operator attempt out of the chain's past can also do it; that
  is a 51% attack on the chain, not a lane-A lever.
* **The capacity loop.** Operator attempts need room; room frees on Final; Final needs binding; binding
  needs operator attempts. A seat at capacity in steady state has about 20/145 of its claims waiting
  for an anchor, so it keeps producing as claims Final. The loop can only close if bound claims fail to
  licence en masse (a silent-seat attack or a seat outage): redraws re-base claims to `Provisional`, the
  seats fill with waiting claims and stop producing. The backstop then voids them (S0) within 580 DAA.
  **Node-side mitigation (a decision, not implemented):** operator producers keep one slot of headroom
  and spend it only on an anchor, when a claim's slot has passed with no operator attempt.

### Fence mechanics

The four places and the rest, as F1: the field; `palw_fences_v1` (the height, so the fork id and the
schedule walk see it); `for_each_fence` Some-only; Some-only writes in `consensus_schedule_id` (name +
height) and `consensus_params_id` (name, height, `PALW_OPERATOR_ANCHOR_DOMAIN_V1`, the operator count and
outpoints — two builds trusting different operators at one height announce different rulesets; keys are
not written, they are the genesis registrations already in the ruleset id); the `Some(never())` collapse,
list and all, in `normalize_values_a_scheduled_fence_drags_with_it`; `override_params` carries it; the
fork-id probe; the extension's ruleset candidate. Dormant on every preset: testnet-12 stays `b8564b88…`
/ `5de80e64…` / `93da24cc…` (pinned in `palw_operator_anchor_is_t12_only.rs`, registered in
`scripts/t12_repin.py` as `opanchor.T12_RELEASE`). The displacement fix changed no `Params` field and
moves no fingerprint: `sw8_anchor_reach` is a runtime input to the fold, `None` wherever lane A is not
in force.

### Tests

* **Pure:** `palw_operator_anchor_v1::tests` — the list is the genesis registry, sorted, and refused
  when empty, unsorted, duplicated or foreign; the header half of the rule on both attempt lanes below
  and past the fence, a genesis bond off the list, a stranger, an impersonation, a heartbeat, a garbled
  envelope.
* **Params** (`palw_operator_anchor_is_t12_only.rs`): dormant everywhere and testnet-12 is the release;
  testnet-12's operators are its eight cards under their registered keys; armed (with F1) it moves params
  and schedule ids, not the identity, and the params id names the list; `Some(never())` is absence;
  genesis-armed separates identities; the fork id at one common height and at a height above F1's;
  refusals by name.
* **Processor** (`t12_operator_anchor_fence`, harness cards 0–5 as the operators, 6–7 as registered
  non-operators):
  - the crossing (released vs lane A + F1 at `3 × anchor_delay`; identical roots below the fence; three
    non-operator attempts past it anchor nothing — `palw_sw8_anchor_delay_for` is `None` on each; the
    straddling claim and the non-operators' own claims bind at operator attempts; a second node agrees);
  - the backstop (only non-operator attempts reach a slot: `BindTimeout` at `bind_base + 600`, not
    before);
  - `displacing_an_operator_attempt_binds_the_claim_in_the_next_block_on_the_displaced_draw` (finding
    1's probe, flipped): five races in which a non-operator sibling wins the tie-break over the
    operator's attempt at a claim's slot, with a heartbeat, a non-operator's attempt and another
    operator's attempt as the next chain block, operator and non-operator claimants, and the claimant
    displacing its own anchor. Each time the next block merges the displaced attempt and binds the claim
    there, on the displaced attempt's seed, with the anchor fact's `anchor_daa` at the displaced
    attempt's DAA (finding 2), and with the seats the displaced attempt drew while it was the chain;
    nothing is voided;
  - `a_block_merging_a_displaced_operator_attempt_anchors_only_the_slots_it_reached`: an operator
    attempt at X's slot, kept off the chain until a heartbeat past Y's slot merges it, binds X there
    (drawn at the attempt's DAA) and neither binds nor voids Y, which binds at the next operator attempt
    on its own seed. With `sw8_anchor_reach` removed, Y is voided `BindTimeout` (mutation-checked).
* **Attacker:** `void_after_panel_probe` T3c and `void_after_panel_adv_verify` a5 (above).

### What the drill across the fence must add (to the F1 list)

1. A non-operator producer on the drill network (a registered bond outside the list): past the height its
   attempts are chain blocks, make claims, and anchor nothing unless they merge an operator attempt; its
   claims bind at operator attempts.
2. Every `PanelBound` past the height names a seed recomputed off-node from the earliest operator attempt
   at or past the claim's slot that the binding block is or merges.
3. Liveness through the crossing with the drill's operator producers throttled to the live rate.
4. If the harness can stage it, one displacement (a non-operator sibling winning the tie-break): the
   claim binds in the next chain block, on the displaced attempt's seed.

### Monitoring (C), to build beside it

* Per claim: the binding block, the seed-source operator attempt, the binding block's DAA minus the
  seed source's DAA, and the seated operators. A seed source that is not the binding block is a
  displacement (or a late merge).
* **Alarm on displacement**: any operator attempt at or past a pending slot that ends up merged off the
  selected chain. Alarm harder on repeated displacement by the same producer, and on any seed source
  merged more than one chain block after it was published (someone kept it out of a chain block's
  past).
* Per operator pair: how often operator X's attempts seat operator Y, against the stake-weighted
  expectation.
